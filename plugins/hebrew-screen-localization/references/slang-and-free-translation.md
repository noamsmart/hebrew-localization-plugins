# Slang, conversational meaning and free contextual translation

Translate what the expression does in the scene into natural Hebrew. This applies to slang, idioms, ordinary conversational expressions, flirtation, teasing, sarcasm, elliptical replies and meaningful written text. A phrase need not be dictionary-labelled slang to require contextual adaptation. Free translation gives freedom over wording, not over the facts, intensity, participants or story.

## Decide the intended sense before choosing words

1. Read the complete exchange and inspect available picture, delivery, gesture, written prop and reaction. Identify the speaker or text author, addressee, referent, relationship and what each participant knows at that moment. Use source evidence and scoped user corrections; do not make up an unseen context.
2. Identify the communicative act: a request for reassurance, flirtation, refusal, dismissal, threat, insult, genuine question, irony, evasion or another supported act. Determine which sense of an ambiguous expression fits the exchange. Words such as like, good, fine, whatever and right have no single mandatory Hebrew equivalent.
3. Draft the complete meaning in Hebrew, then choose concise wording that this character or writer would naturally use. Preserve the act, emotional force, politeness, certainty, question/answer relationship and any intentional awkwardness. Do not substitute slang merely to make every character sound casual.
4. Compare the adaptation with the source: who does what to whom; negation and its scope; conditions, quantities, tense, certainty, intention, emotional degree and disclosure order. A smoother sentence fails if it turns liking into love, a possibility into a fact, a refusal into acceptance, or a mistaken belief into correct knowledge.
5. Recheck the full linked exchange after subtitle condensation or dubbing adaptation. If material context remains unavailable, flag the competing senses for contextual review; choose a natural formulation that preserves supported ambiguity where possible. Do not silently resolve an important uncertainty through invented wording.

## Context-dependent examples, not a replacement dictionary

| Source expression and evidenced situation | Suitable Hebrew example | What must be preserved |
|---|---|---|
| A girl writes “Do you like me?” on a flirtatious questionnaire | **אני מוצאת חן בעיניך?** | First-person female writer, romantic interest and a question. In the Gravity Falls card this is the user-approved exact form; retain אני rather than shortening it away. |
| “Do you like me?” asks about ordinary friendship or personal liking | אתה מחבב אותי? / את מחבבת אותי? | Relationship, addressee agreement and the actual degree of affection. Do not automatically escalate like to אוהב/אוהבת. |
| “I'm good” politely declines an offer | לא, תודה. | Refusal, rather than a claim of skill or wellbeing. |
| “I'm good” answers a question about wellbeing | אני בסדר. | The supported health/emotional meaning and certainty. |
| “I'm good at this” describes ability | אני טוב בזה. / אני טובה בזה. | Ability, speaker agreement and the referent of this. |
| “Shut up!” is delighted disbelief between friends | די, לא נכון! | Excitement or disbelief; do not add a hostile command. When the source really orders silence, use an appropriately forceful Hebrew command instead. |
| “Don't mind if I do” accepts an invitation playfully | בשמחה. | Acceptance and tone, rather than a literal discussion of whether anyone minds. |
| “Whatever” dismisses another person's claim | מה שתגיד. / מה שתגידי. | Dismissive attitude and the actual addressee. A sincere statement that any option is acceptable needs different wording. |

Choose another Hebrew form when the evidence calls for it. These examples do not grant permission for global string replacement. Match slang to genre, period, age, social setting, relationship and the local emotional beat. Preserve register changes supported by the scene. Keep an adult character's source intensity and a child's actual voice; do not add profanity, sexual meaning, fashionable slang or sanitization absent from the source. Do not relocate the story to Israel. Follow `names-and-titles.md` separately for names, titles, brands and sentence-shaped entities.

## Written text, jokes and literal wording that matters

Apply the same sense selection to cards, notes, chats, signs and letters. Preserve the author's voice separately from a reader's voice. Check what the writing says, what the character reads or claims, and how another character reacts; a fluent rewrite must not make those three events agree when the source deliberately makes them differ. Keep visible question/answer choices and gradual reveals in their original order. A text correction changes wording only unless visual evidence also justifies a timing change.

Identify joke setup, payoff, repeated catchphrase and relevant prop/gesture before adapting one line. When a joke bridges dialogue and writing, solve the whole group together: a natural idiom that breaks the visible gag is insufficient. Retain deliberate malapropisms, misreadings, unfinished wording and their later corrections. A broken or clumsy line may be intentional; do not repair away its function. Where an exact word is a clue, quotation, password, number, name or repeatable punchline, preserve that anchor and adapt the surrounding sentence rather than replacing it freely.

## Review and scoped memory

Perform a contextual naturalness pass over all selected dialogue **and meaningful writing**, not only the user's example or highlighted slang words. Then run the existing source-fidelity gate after every material free rewrite and compression. Review connected turns, written stages and joke payoffs. Fluent Hebrew, a dictionary lookup, back-translation, automated checks and a saved decision do not prove semantic or audiovisual quality.

For material or recurring adaptations, reuse the existing bible/event ledger. Record adaptation kind (slang/idiom/pragmatic sense/wordplay), source/context evidence, the intended act preserved, approved Hebrew wording, linked cue/visual/reaction IDs, scope and unresolved questions. Do not create a record for every ordinary word. User-approved exact wording takes priority within its scope and must survive shortening; generalizing a one-scene correction to another situation still requires compatible context.
