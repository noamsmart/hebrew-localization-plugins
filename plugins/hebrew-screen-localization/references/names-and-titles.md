# Names and titles: identify before translating

Words can describe something or identify a named entity. Decide which function they serve in the current scene before choosing Hebrew wording. A name remains a name even when it is grammatically a complete sentence; translating its individual words can erase its recognizable identity while producing perfectly fluent Hebrew.

## Decision process

1. Identify the referent from nearby dialogue, logos/title cards, introduction/promotion context, the work as a whole and the continuity bible. Programme, film, series, book and song titles, brands, organizations, products and proper names require this check. Capitalization, quotation marks, an ASR transcript or a filename alone are not conclusive.
2. Use the explicit user/project-approved Hebrew form for that referent. Otherwise use a verified established Hebrew name/title when one is known; if none is established, choose a readable, consistent transliteration (or source-script form required by the delivery specification). Do not invent a semantic title by translating the component words. This does not prohibit established Hebrew titles that are translations. Do not claim a form is official or established without evidence.
3. Record the approved spelling, referent, genuine aliases/abbreviations, decision basis and scope in the continuity bible. Preserve identity across speech and selected written text, including prepositions and inflections around the name. Do not merge similar titles, franchises or spin-offs; resolve the full title and any suffix in its own context.
4. Translate an ordinary descriptive use normally, even when the same words also occur in a title. A playful nickname or comic description can be adapted naturally when that is its contextual function. Do not classify every capitalized expression or nickname as an immutable brand.
5. If dialogue deliberately exploits a title's literal meaning, preserve the identifiable name and the joke together, within the source's meaning and the reading limits. Preserve deliberate misnaming and its correction when the story or joke needs them. If a material dual reading cannot be resolved, mark it for contextual review rather than silently picking one.

## User-approved programme example

When `Britain's Got Talent` identifies the programme, use **בריטניה גוט טאלנט**, as explicitly requested by the user. Do not call the programme **לבריטניה יש כישרון**. This is a user-approved name choice, not a claim about an official Hebrew distribution title. `BGT` maps to this name only where context establishes the same programme. The general lesson is to protect named identity, not to force this spelling onto other works or every mention of Britain and talent.

Context examples:
- Programme reference: “Do you think you can win Britain's Got Talent?” → “לדעתך יש לך סיכוי לזכות בבריטניה גוט טאלנט?”
- Programme introduction: “Welcome to Britain's Got Talent.” → “ברוכים הבאים לבריטניה גוט טאלנט.”
- Descriptive statement with no programme reference: “Britain has a lot of talent.” → “יש בבריטניה המון כישרונות.”
- A different work with a verified or user-approved translated Hebrew title: retain that title; do not transliterate it merely because this programme example uses a transliteration.
- A joke quoting a programme name and then discussing its literal meaning: keep the approved programme name and adapt the meaning-related joke in context; do not silently substitute a newly invented title.

## Required review

After drafting, perform a separate identity pass over all selected dialogue and on-screen text. Fluency, free adaptation and correct dictionary meanings cannot establish that a name is correct. Match each occurrence and true alias to its intended entity and approved form. Check surrounding grammar, RTL, line breaks and reading load after a correction.

When a user corrects a name, inspect affected prior occurrences with source/context and record IDs/timecodes. Never apply a blind global replacement to ordinary uses of the same words. Keep existing exported tracks and encoded videos marked as needing correction until they have actually been regenerated and checked; updating this policy alone does not repair a prior deliverable.
