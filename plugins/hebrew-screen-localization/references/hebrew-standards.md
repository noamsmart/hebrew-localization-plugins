# Hebrew localization standards

Use these as professional defaults unless the user or delivery platform supplies stricter requirements.

## Language

- Translate meaning, intent, subtext and characterization rather than source-language syntax.
- Produce idiomatic contemporary Hebrew suitable for the characters and setting.
- Prefer freer, natural spoken Hebrew for slang, idioms, playful nicknames and comic labels when it preserves the source meaning, intended effect and character voice. Slang is context-dependent; it is not required in every line.
- Review all selected dialogue for stiff calques and overly literal phrasing, including recurring setups and payoffs; apply the semantic gate after every rewrite.
- Preserve differences in register, age, education, intimacy, status, humor, profanity and dialect without making every character sound identical.
- Resolve gender, number and forms of address from audiovisual context while preserving what has been disclosed to the audience at that moment; use natural neutral phrasing when agreement would reveal intentionally withheld information.
- Classify names/titles by their function in audiovisual context before translating; a title that resembles a sentence still identifies a work or brand. Follow `names-and-titles.md`.
- Preserve named identity using the user/project-approved Hebrew form, otherwise a verified established Hebrew name/title or consistent transliteration. Do not invent a literal Hebrew title; an established translated title remains valid.
- Keep approved names, genuine aliases and recurring terms consistent across an episode and series, while translating ordinary descriptive uses normally.
- Avoid explanatory additions that the source does not communicate.

## Subtitle defaults

Netflix's current Hebrew timed-text guidance is a useful industry benchmark, not a universal legal requirement. Relevant defaults include up to 42 characters per line and no more than two lines. General guidance also sets event-duration limits and emphasizes timing to audio and shot changes. For SDH, Netflix currently gives adult and children's reading-speed ceilings of 20 and 17 characters per second respectively. Use platform-specific specifications when delivering to another service.

Treat 20 CPS as a hard warning threshold for ordinary adult Hebrew subtitles unless a target platform specifies otherwise; aim lower when a natural condensation can preserve meaning.

Line breaks must follow Hebrew grammar and reading units. Do not split tightly bound phrases merely to balance line length.

## RTL delivery

For professional TTML/IMSC delivery, explicitly encode RTL direction according to the target specification. Do not assume file extension alone guarantees correct RTL rendering.

## On-screen text

Apply the on-screen text selection rules in `../skills/dialogue-selection/SKILL.md` to narrative cards and meaningful written information. Omit duplicate cues only when the same relevant information is already conveyed clearly at the appropriate moment without losing a clue, joke or disclosure order. When dialogue and on-screen text compete for limited reading time, prioritize what the viewer needs to follow the story and explicitly resolve any essential information that cannot fit.
