# Dubbing adaptation

A good subtitle script is not automatically a good dubbing script.

Rewrite Hebrew for speakability, breath, emotional beat and approximate utterance duration. For close-up dialogue, favor phrasing that better fits visible mouth openings/closures without sacrificing meaning or naturalness. For off-screen lines, prioritize performance and timing over lip matching.

Preserve character voice and scene rhythm. Mark overlaps, interruptions, efforts, reactions and performance cues only when useful to actors/directors.

Audio rule: do not simply place Hebrew dialogue over clearly audible source dialogue. Prefer a clean M&E track. If unavailable, require dialogue/source separation, inspect leakage and artifacts scene by scene, and do not call the mix professional while obvious source voices remain.

Background source speech that is intentionally part of the scene must be handled deliberately: translate/cast it when narratively required, or keep it as ambience only when the creative intent permits.
