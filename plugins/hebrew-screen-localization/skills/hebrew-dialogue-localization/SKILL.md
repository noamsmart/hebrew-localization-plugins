# Hebrew dialogue localization

Write Hebrew that feels authored in Hebrew while remaining faithful to the work.

For each segment, preserve: factual meaning, emotional intent, relationship dynamics, subtext, joke mechanism, information order when dramatically important and the character's register.

Identify the line's communicative act, such as a threat, tease, evasion, reassurance or reconciliation, and preserve its force and register. Choose Hebrew wording from the scene, delivery and speaker rather than a fixed dictionary equivalent. Preserve what a character claims, including lies and mistaken beliefs; do not fact-correct the dialogue using later knowledge.

Before translating a phrase, classify its function in context: ordinary dialogue, a named entity/title, or deliberate wordplay on a name. A programme or brand name remains a name even when its words form a complete sentence. Follow `../../references/names-and-titles.md`: use the user/project-approved form, otherwise a verified established Hebrew name or consistent transliteration. Do not turn a name into an invented literal Hebrew title, and do not transliterate ordinary descriptive speech merely because the same words can be a title. Keep this identity check separate from the naturalness/slang pass; playful descriptive nicknames still require contextual adaptation.

Do not mirror English or another source language's word order when Hebrew would sound stiff. Condense where subtitle readability requires it, but remove redundancy before removing meaning.

Prefer natural, spoken Hebrew and a freer contextual adaptation over a literal equivalent when it preserves the source's meaning and intended effect. Actively adapt slang, idioms, teasing nicknames and comic labels to the scene, speaker and audience. For example, when a girl blowing bubble gum is playfully addressed as "bubble girl", prefer "ילדת מסטיק" over "ילדת הבועות". This is a context-specific example, not a fixed replacement for every mention of bubbles.

After drafting, review all selected dialogue for stiff calques and overly literal phrasing; do not limit this naturalness pass to the user's example or a few sampled lines. Choose idiomatic Hebrew that carries the same intent, humor and character voice. Use slang where the character, era and situation support it, without adding profanity, fashionable expressions or information absent from the source. Preserve wording needed for recurring jokes and callbacks by adapting the setup and payoff together. Apply the source-fidelity checks below after each freer rewrite.

After every condensation, adaptation or correction, compare the Hebrew directly with the source and verify negation and its scope, conditions and exceptions, quantities and numbers, tense, certainty or probability, and who does what to whom. Fluency or back-translation alone does not establish fidelity. For example, "He might come" must not become "הוא יבוא".

Maintain distinct character voices. Track recurring forms of address, profanity level, catchphrases, technical vocabulary and intentional grammatical quirks.

Preserve deliberate mistakes, malapropisms and mispronunciations when a joke or plot point depends on them. Adapt the mistaken line and its correction together so the mechanism works in Hebrew. Do not silently repair a meaningful error, mechanically copy source-language defects, or add broken Hebrew where it serves no function.

Use the full audiovisual context to understand gender, number, identity and relationships, but keep each line within what the source has disclosed to the audience at that moment. Preserve intentional ambiguity; do not reveal a later identity, gender or family relationship through Hebrew agreement or added explanation. Prefer natural neutral phrasing when needed. Do not conceal information the source already conveys. Flag a material ambiguity that cannot be preserved for contextual review.

Treat jokes, idioms, wordplay and cultural references as localization problems. Recreate the function of the line rather than translating components literally; document major adaptations in the continuity bible.

Adapt idioms and humor without relocating the story to Israel. Keep source places, brands and public figures identifiable through established Hebrew names, the same brand's established local name, or transliteration. Do not substitute unrelated Israeli equivalents merely for familiarity.

Track separately what the audience is meant to understand and what each character understands when languages switch. Preserve language barriers, misunderstandings and their dramatic consequences. Translate speech for the audience when dialogue-selection requires it even if a character does not understand it; do not add an explanation or alter a reaction to make that character appear informed. Do not automatically omit foreign-language dialogue or translate intentionally opaque speech merely because it can be transcribed.

Use `../../references/hebrew-standards.md` as the default language standard. Send the translated text to subtitle or dubbing adaptation rather than treating the raw translation as final.

## Character and written-context pass

Use `../character-voice-review/SKILL.md` and `../../references/character-voice-and-address.md` to ground each character's voice in evidence and allow intentional changes by listener, scene and emotion. Track speaker, addressee, referent and quoted speaker separately before choosing Hebrew agreement. Review complete exchanges after cue-level drafting and condensation; a neutral rewrite must preserve the actual participants and information.

For reading or responding to messages/signs/documents, use `../written-text-context/SKILL.md`. Preserve distinctions among literal reading, paraphrase, mistake, concealment and performed claims. Do not make a character know text merely because the viewer sees it; retain the text author's style separately from the reader's speaking voice. Check linked written event, spoken line and reaction together, with their original reveal times.

## Slang and conversational sense selection

Use `../../references/slang-and-free-translation.md` to select the meaning of the complete expression from the exchange before choosing Hebrew words. Extend the naturalness pass to all selected dialogue and meaningful writing, including common conversational phrases that are not strict slang. Preserve the speech act, relational/emotional degree, writer/speaker voice and any literal anchors needed by a visible joke. Recheck source fidelity after every free rewrite and subtitle/dubbing compression. Apply user-approved exact wording within its recorded context; do not shorten away an explicitly requested word or generalize a local correction by string replacement.
